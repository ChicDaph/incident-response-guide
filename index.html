What To Do If You Suspect You Have Been Scammed

        
General Consumer Cybersecurity Guidance Article

      


      

        

          Online fraud and social engineering attacks take many forms, including phishing emails, smishing text messages, tech support scams, and fraudulent payment requests. If you believe you may have interacted with a scammer, it is important to take steps to protect your accounts, devices, and financial well-being. Because scams vary widely depending on the type of information exposed, you should carefully read through all sections below to identify which advice applies to your situation.
        


        
1. Financial Accounts and Payment Transfers

        

          If you paid a scammer with a credit or debit card, contact your card issuer immediately using the phone number printed on the back of your physical card and ask them to reverse the charge or freeze the account. If you sent money via wire transfer, peer-to-peer payment apps (such as Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App), cryptocurrency, or gift cards, contact the payment provider or gift card company right away to report fraud, though recovery options vary by payment method. If you shared your Social Security Number or tax identification details, consider placing a security freeze with all three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion) and placing a fraud alert on your credit file.
        


        
2. Compromised Passwords and Login Credentials

        

          If you entered your username, password, or multi-factor authentication code on a suspicious website, change your password immediately using a separate, trusted device. Ensure that your new password is strong and unique. If you reused that same password on other websites—especially email or banking accounts—update those passwords as well. Enable app-based multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever available and review your account settings to sign out of all active sessions or unrecognized devices.
        


        
3. Malicious Downloads and Remote Access Software

        

          If you downloaded an unknown attachment, installed software from an unverified source, or allowed someone to remotely access your computer or phone, disconnect the device from the internet immediately (turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the Ethernet cable) to prevent further unauthorized access. Uninstall any remote access utilities you were instructed to install, update your security software, and run a full antivirus and anti-malware scan. If the device belongs to your employer or school, notify your organization's IT or Information Security department immediately so they can isolate and inspect the system.
        


        
4. Suspicious Links and Unsolicited Replies

        

          If you only clicked a link without entering credentials or downloading files, close the browser tab, clear your browser cache and cookies, and ensure your browser and operating system are up to date. If you replied to a scam message or shared your phone number or email address, be alert for follow-up phishing attempts or impersonation calls, and avoid engaging further with the sender.
        


        
5. Documenting Evidence and Reporting Fraud

        

          Preserve evidence of the interaction by taking screenshots of messages, email headers, phone numbers, or transaction receipts without clicking any links inside the suspicious message. Report the scam to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov, and your local law enforcement agency if financial loss or identity theft occurred.
